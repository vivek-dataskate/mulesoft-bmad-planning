The Challenge
Why this matters now
▼

'This is an ancient system. I feel like I've traveled back to the 90s.' That's the current state at Peerless — and it's also the daily routine: Ashley manually checks Home Depot reporting to find deposit status by hand, then spends 1.5 hours rekeying every closed Salesforce quote into ComputerEase, then reviews each contract document one by one against the rule set she has memorized. That stops on go-live day.

The 1.5-hour rekey

Every time a Salesforce quote is marked 'Save as Final,' Ashley opens ComputerEase and rebuilds the job by hand — customer number, the sequential job number (company code + product line + initials, e.g. '26 HD 565 AS'), and the full budget worksheet. She walked us through it on 2026-04-16: roughly 1.5 hours per closed quote, every closed quote.

Reading every document by hand

Ashley reviews each signed HS-105, 299A, 299B, and HS-118 one at a time against a rule set she has memorized — prices on the 299A and 299B must match, signatures must be legible, dates must fall on a Friday, the yes/no boxes can't both be ticked, and Wisconsin deposits must hit 99% where everywhere else is 100%.

Why this isn't a Salesforce-native build

The ComputerEase connection isn't a REST API call — it's a Deltek-managed relay URL routed through CE Live Service, running on port 445, accessible only in production, with a sequential job-number consumption rule that requires calling Deltek's next-id endpoint before every write. A Salesforce-native Flows/Apex approach hits an architectural wall at the ComputerEase layer. DataSkate named this before the engagement started.
