Corporate Profile — Deep Research

1. Origin & History

Peerless Fence Group traces its roots to 1961, when Art White and Robert White founded Peerless Erectors in the western Chicago suburbs — a family fence contracting business that Robert's son Dean would grow into the largest independently owned fence contractor in the Midwest. From the 1960s through the 2000s, Peerless remained a single-brand regional operator known for residential and commercial fencing across Illinois.

The first phase of strategic transformation came between 2013 and 2019, when Dean White's Peerless began a self-funded regional rollup: Tru-Link Fence, Imperial Fence Company, Shogren Fence, IFFT Quality Fencing, Total Fencing, Link-n-Wood Fence Co., Woodland Fence, and The Fence Store were acquired in succession. By 2019, Peerless operated across Illinois, Indiana, Ohio, and southern Wisconsin — a fully integrated regional contractor with 60+ years of family ownership and a decade of consolidation behind it.

The second transformation came on January 1, 2023: Akoya Capital Partners, a Chicago-based middle-market private equity firm founded in 2005, acquired Peerless Fence Group as a platform investment in its Residential Home Services thesis. This marked the end of the family-ownership-only era. Akoya's stated investment range is $25M–$200M revenue middle-market companies — Peerless sits toward the lower end of that band.

The third transformation, announced February 5, 2024: Dean White transitioned from CEO to Executive Chairman, and Roger Greenhagel (previously VP of Material Sales at Peerless, formerly National Account Sales Manager at Barrette Outdoor Living) was appointed President — a classic PE-era professional-management transition. Founder Dean White remains actively involved as Executive Chairman; the operating role now runs through Greenhagel.

Concurrent with the Akoya acquisition, Peerless joined Elevate Fence Partners — a national fence-contractor platform that operates six regional brands across the Midwest, Southeast, Northeast, and a nationwide e-commerce channel. The relationship is described as a strategic alliance rather than a direct acquisition: Dean White partnered with Elevate to scale Peerless from a respected Midwest regional contractor into a growing national brand. Within the Elevate structure, Peerless is the Midwest anchor.

2. Business Model

Peerless Fence Group is a specialty residential fence contractor — aluminum, wood, vinyl, and chain-link — serving homeowners across Illinois, Indiana, Ohio, and southern Wisconsin. Six locations: St. Charles (HQ), West Chicago, Round Lake, Bloomington (all IL), Greenwood (IN), Cincinnati and Delaware (OH).

A material share of revenue flows through the Home Depot Pro-contractor channel: leads originate in Home Depot Service Center (HD Portal), flow into Salesforce CRM for quoting and contract management, then move to ComputerEase (Deltek) for job costing and accounting. Revenue estimates range from $15M to $32M annually, positioning Peerless firmly in the midmarket segment. A flagship commercial win was announced in October 2025: Peerless secured the contract to install 10,600+ linear feet of fencing at the Obama Presidential Center in Chicago — evidence of commercial capacity beyond the HD residential channel.

Two dynamics define the integration scope. First, the HD Pro channel is the highest-volume lead source and the most structurally constrained: HD Portal operates on its own API cadence (10-minute update cycle), its own lead identifier format (F-number, not a generic ID), its own contract documents (HS-105, 299A, 299B, HS-118), and its own write-back channel. Every new state expansion adds a new HD Portal footprint layer and state-specific deposit/permit rules. Second, ComputerEase (Deltek, version ~26.x) is a DOS-era ERP whose API surface only exists in the production tenant (no sandbox), is only accessible via a Deltek-managed relay URL routed through the CE Live Service Windows service, and is administered by a single super-admin (Jean Jacobs). These two facts together define the hardest architectural choices in the current engagement.
