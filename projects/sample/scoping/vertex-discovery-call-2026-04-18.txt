Vertex Cloud Solutions — Discovery Call
Date: April 18, 2026
Attendees: Sarah Chen (VP Revenue Operations), Derek Okafor (Finance Manager), Kailash Chanda (DataSkate), Vivek Yadlapalli (DataSkate)

---

KAILASH: Sarah, thanks for making time. Can you walk us through what a typical deal close looks like today — from the moment it goes to Closed-Won in HubSpot to the moment the customer gets an invoice?

SARAH: Sure. So deal closes in HubSpot, the rep marks it Closed-Won. That triggers… nothing. Literally nothing automated. Somebody on the sales team sends a Slack message or an email to Derek's team saying hey, we have a new customer. Derek or someone on his team then logs into NetSuite, creates the customer record, creates the subscription, creates the invoice. That whole process is at least 48 hours minimum. I've seen it take four or five days if Derek's team is swamped.

DEREK: And we're closing roughly 80 new logos a year right now. So that's 80 times we're doing this manually. It's fine when we're small but we just hired three new AEs and the volume is about to go up.

VIVEK: What's the business cost of the 48-hour lag? Have you ever tried to put a number on it?

SARAH: I haven't done the exact calculation but it's real. At our average ACV, if 80 deals each slip 48 hours on invoice creation, and we're running net-30 terms, that's meaningful deferred revenue. I'd say it's pushing $400K in deferred ARR recognition per quarter if you do the math. That's my estimate.

KAILASH: And what about visibility going the other way — does your sales team know when invoices are paid?

SARAH: That's the other problem. Account managers live in HubSpot. Invoice payment status lives in NetSuite. The only way an AM knows if a customer is overdue is if Derek's team tells them, or they stumble onto it on a renewal call. We've had situations where an AM is on a renewal call and finds out the customer is 90 days overdue — in front of the customer. That's not great.

DEREK: We had a customer churn last quarter partly because the overdue situation wasn't caught until it was too late. The AM had no visibility.

SARAH: And renewals. We've lost two renewals this year because nobody was tracking the 90-day window. The contract end date is in NetSuite. The opportunity needs to be created in HubSpot. There's no connection. So the window just… passes.

VIVEK: What does the Slack notification situation look like today?

SARAH: Non-existent for most of this. Finance will sometimes ping the ops Slack channel when they create the NetSuite record, but it's manual, it's inconsistent. There's no systematic alerting. We have a #revenue-ops channel that's basically used for ad-hoc messages.

KAILASH: Okay. Let me describe what we'd build and you tell me if it maps to what you're describing. Flow one: when a deal moves to Closed-Won in HubSpot, MuleSoft automatically creates the NetSuite customer record, the subscription, and the first invoice — in minutes, not 48 hours. Does that solve the first problem?

SARAH: Yes. That's exactly it. Can you do it that fast?

KAILASH: Yes. HubSpot has a webhook that fires on deal stage change. We catch that event and trigger the NetSuite creation. The only dependency is that the deal record in HubSpot has the fields we need — company name, deal value, subscription term. We confirm those in the field mapping session.

SARAH: The AEs are pretty good about filling those in. We have required fields for Closed-Won already.

KAILASH: Flow two: invoice payment status syncs back from NetSuite to HubSpot. Your AMs see billing health on the deal record — no NetSuite login required. Overdue accounts surface automatically, not on the renewal call.

DEREK: That would help us enormously. We spend a non-trivial amount of time on questions from AMs asking where invoices stand. If that's visible in HubSpot we don't get those questions.

SARAH: And renewal dates?

KAILASH: Yes — contract end date syncs to HubSpot, renewal opportunity auto-creates 90 days out. Third flow: billing events — subscription created, invoice overdue, renewal approaching — fire automated Slack messages to the right channels. #ops-alerts, #finance-ops, #revenue-ops. Whoever you want.

SARAH: Finally. Yes. That's what we need. This is exactly what we need.

VIVEK: Sarah, one question before we talk timeline — eventually what do you want AI to be doing for you on top of this data?

SARAH: Honestly? I want it to tell me which accounts are at risk before they tell me. I don't want to be reactive. If an account hasn't had any HubSpot activity in 30 days and has an overdue invoice, that's a signal. I want that surfaced automatically, not discovered on the renewal call.

DEREK: And I want the weekly reconciliation to go away. Every Monday I build a spreadsheet that reconciles HubSpot pipeline against NetSuite actuals. It takes me two to three hours. If that ran automatically and just flagged the exceptions, I would have my Monday mornings back.

KAILASH: Both of those are Year 2. And they only work if the Year 1 data foundation is clean — which is exactly what we're building.

SARAH: I like how you frame it. What's the ROI calculation? Can you put a number on it?

KAILASH: Your own estimate is $400K in deferred ARR per quarter from the invoice lag. The managed service rate for three flows is $2,475 a month. The business case for moving now is pretty simple.

SARAH: What's the payback period?

KAILASH: If the invoice lag alone represents $400K quarterly, you cover the first year of the managed service in the first month of operations. That's not how I'd frame the business case formally, but directionally that's the math.

SARAH: Derek, you're good with the NetSuite access question? Kailash, what do you need from us on that?

KAILASH: We need REST Web Services enabled in your NetSuite instance — that's a toggle in Setup. And we'll need an integration record with OAuth credentials. Your NetSuite admin can handle both in about 20 minutes. That's the only technical prerequisite on your side.

DEREK: Our admin is responsive. I can get that done this week if we move forward.

SARAH: What does the timeline look like?

KAILASH: Seven weeks from signed SOW. Two weeks requirements, four weeks build and test, one week UAT and go-live. You'd be live before August.

SARAH: That works. We want to have this in place before the new AE class is fully ramped.

VIVEK: Great. We'll send a proposal this week. Sarah, the intake form will have a field mapping section — your finance team will need to sit with your AEs for about 20 minutes to confirm the HubSpot fields we'll map to NetSuite. That's the main ask before we can start design.

SARAH: Understood. Let's do it.
